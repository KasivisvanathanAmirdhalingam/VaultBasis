Evidence Receipt Guide

A clear, human-readable reference explaining what a VaultBasis Evidence Receipt is, how reconciliation outcomes are structured, and how receipts are independently verified.

What is a VaultBasis Evidence Receipt?

A VaultBasis Evidence Receipt is a portable, tamper-evident record documenting the outcome of a digital-asset tax reconciliation. It records exactly which evidence files VaultBasis compared, what matched, what differed, what remained unresolved, and whether the receipt has been modified since it was cryptographically signed.

Practitioners include Evidence Receipts in tax workpapers to establish clear, defensible review chronology for returns involving digital-asset broker reporting (Form 1099-DA) and client tax ledgers.

What Information Does an Evidence Receipt Contain?

Every Evidence Receipt contains seven authoritative structural sections:

1. Case & Taxpayer Context Unique Case ID, client engagement reference, tax year, and applicable regulatory jurisdiction (e.g. US IRS).
2. Sources Compared & Hashes Cryptographic SHA-256 digests and file metadata for every ingested source (e.g., broker Form 1099-DA and client ledger).
3. Reconciliation Outcome State Authoritative high-level outcome (MATCHED, PROCEEDS_DIFFERENCE, BASIS_DIFFERENCE, or UNRESOLVED).
4. Material Differences Itemized discrepancies detected between broker reporting and client tax records with rule citations and variance amounts.
5. Unresolved Items Non-covered assets, missing acquisition dates, or unlinked lots that cannot be coerced to $0.00 and require practitioner review.
6. Issuance Timestamp & Engine Metadata Strict UTC ISO 8601 timestamp of reconciliation, ruleset version, and deterministic engine version.
7. Ed25519 Cryptographic Signature & Public Key Digital signature binding the entire payload to the local installation key, preventing retroactive tampering.

How Independent Verification Works

Evidence Receipts are designed to be verified independently by any auditor, CPA, reviewer, or client without needing a VaultBasis subscription or software installation.

The verifier recomputes the SHA-256 digests of all referenced evidence files, checks schema conformance, validates the ruleset binding, and verifies the Ed25519 digital signature. If even a single comma or dollar value is altered, verification fails immediately.
